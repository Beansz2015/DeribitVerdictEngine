## 2. Decisions queued, with my read

**First, what is *not* yours.** Three D-tables are awaiting the **trader**: the coverage report (D1–D7), the overlay (D1–D7), and the v64 test-and-push gate. Do not spend budget on them. The items below are the ones that need a *design* ruling, and each has reach beyond the spec it came from.

### J-A · Ratify the fixture-mirroring rule — **my read: yes, as a standing convention**

F1 is fixed, so this is not about the code. It is about the **second occurrence**. A43f verified a stub's internal arithmetic against hand-computed sums and passed for weeks while the units were wrong; A48f re-stated `ResolveTradeStore`'s gate predicate inline and asserted the copy was false. Different mechanisms, same shape: **the fixture and the production path were consistent with each other and neither was checked against reality.**

**Options:** (a) standing convention — when a gate cannot be exercised directly (private member, live socket, WinForms), **hoist it onto a shared seam and have both production and fixture call it**, never mirror it; (b) case-by-case; (c) nothing.

**My read: (a).** It has now cost twice; the fix is ~10 lines each time; and the project already has the pattern named (`ComputeSideLevels`, "one seam, no copies"). The convention is just that phrase applied to *gates* as well as to *format*.

**Note the disagreement, since it is live:** the implementer's own spec-back §4 reads A48f the other way — *"A48f pins the stronger reading."* For the two-switch asymmetry it describes, that is correct; for the reversibility claim it is attached to, it is not. They fixed it anyway, which is the right outcome, but the reading is unratified.

### J-B · Coverage report C2 — which way does an ambiguous up-interval resolve? — **my read: toward DEFECT**

`ws_health.log` is transition-only, so **"app up and quiet" and "app dead" are byte-identical in the file.** The only disambiguator is GUID continuity on the *next* line, which makes resolution retrospective and leaves the **trailing interval permanently ambiguous** — the interval a daily check cares about most. This is not theoretical: the collector died on 2026-07-31 leaving `OK` as its last line.

**My read: default the ambiguous window to *defect*.** A report that silently reclassifies real loss as expected is worse than no report, and that is the exact failure it exists to prevent. Erring toward defect costs a human dismissal; erring the other way costs tape.

### J-C · Coverage report C5 vs its own D4 — what baseline sets the natural-silence threshold? — **my read: REST-backfilled, contradicting D4**

The spec lists "REST-backfilled rather than streamed" among caveats that *loosen* the threshold. For S3's purpose it cuts the other way: **a REST backfill is the venue's own record of what printed** — the ground truth for *"what is a natural silence?"* A streamed sample contains capture-path gaps **in addition**, so re-anchoring on it (D4's plan) would **inflate** the threshold by absorbing capture gaps into the "natural" baseline, blunting the signal.

**My read:** keep deriving the natural-silence baseline from REST-backfilled windows — extended to include a weekend, which is the genuinely load-bearing caveat of the three — and use streamed data to measure the **difference**, because that difference *is* the capture-gap signal.

### J-D · Ratify the overlay's restated whitelist rule — **my read: yes**

Original: *a key is safe to diverge per box **exactly when** it is safe off the tweaker surface.* Elegant, and false — `network.transport` carries HC12 because the tweaker has no business tuning timeouts, **not** because transport is scoring-neutral. Restated after review: **the HC fences are a good first filter, not a proof; where the two justifications diverge, review per key.**

**My read: ratify the restatement as standing.** It has reach past this spec — the same conflation is available anywhere an existing fence gets reused as evidence for a new property. **Caveat I want on the record:** I audited two of eight blocks and both failed; the implementer reports auditing the remaining six and finding them clean. **I did not re-audit their audit** (§4).

### J-E · The effective-source CSV stamp — worth a rotation slot? — **my read: yes**

`SignalEmitter.DeriveWsHealth` already returns `REST`/`DEGRADED`/`DOWN`/`OK` every run and ships in the bridge payload; **none of `analysis_log.csv`'s 111 columns records it.** Meanwhile `ResolveSource` silently returns `_restSource` on a degraded feed, so **REST-scored rows are already in the book, unmarked, at an unmeasured rate.**

**My read: yes — it rides the next natural rotation alongside `TriggerMode` and must never force one.** It converts overlay-D7 option (c) from hypothetical to implementable, makes the degradation rate measurable for the first time, and gives the coverage report's S1 a *positive* per-run state record instead of an inference. Full reasoning: [`overlay-d7-and-row-source-stamp-2026-07-31.md`](overlay-d7-and-row-source-stamp-2026-07-31.md) §4.

### J-F · Shares a root with JOB 2's D-F — rule them together

JOB 2's **D-F** asks whether derivation briefs should mandate a store-completeness pre-flight. **J-B, J-E and D-F are the same question at three altitudes**: how does this project notice that data is missing rather than quiet? D-F is the analyst-side convention, J-B the report's resolution rule, J-E the per-row instrument. Ruling them together is cheaper and avoids three inconsistent answers. **I have no preference on ordering** — only that they not be ruled in isolation.

## 3. Spec-back proper — feedback on the brief

### What the brief got right, specifically

**Naming a prior failure made the check concrete.** *"Do not accept an implementer's claim that a fixture passes — read the fixture and check it tests what its name says. There is a live precedent: A43f … Internal consistency cannot detect a unit error."* That paragraph is what produced F1. A generic "review the fixtures" would not have — I would have confirmed A48a–h existed and passed. The specific failure mode made me read what each one *asserts*, which is where the mirrored predicate was visible.

**"Verify, don't assume" on the fixture-family number** generalised well. I applied the same posture to the §12 rows and to the store's completeness, and both paid.

### Which assumptions broke

**1. Two of the 13 checklist items could not pass as written.** *"Verify no HttpClient reached either [the feed path or the fixture project]."* The app **is supposed** to gain one — §2 of the spec says gap repair *"is the one place the app takes a dependency on the runner's networking, and is why D5 exists,"* and D5 was ruled Yes. And the fixture project **already linked `HistoricalStore` before this build**, so that half was false in both directions independently of anything the implementer did. I reported them as checklist bugs rather than raising false positives, but a less careful reviewer would have logged two defects that were not defects.

**2. "Review what lands in the repo" did not anticipate "lands in the working tree."** The build was uncommitted when I reviewed it. That is not a problem for reading code — but it silently degraded the acceptance evidence: `verify-gate` derives its changed-file set from **committed** diffs, so `display-parity` and `version-bump` both passed **vacuously** while the gate still printed `GATE PASSED`. **The brief treats "GATE PASSED" as a checklist item with a single meaning; it has two.** Worth writing into the next review brief: *require the gate run to be post-commit, or state which of its checks are vacuous.*

**3. The brief scoped JOB 1 to an artifact; the work was a domain.** One build became three specs, two store incidents and a test-debt item — and the two incidents were the highest-value output of JOB 1, while being nowhere in its 13-point checklist. **Suggest scoping review work to a domain with a standing instruction** ("review the trade-store arc; the checklist covers the build") so the reviewer is not implicitly out of scope when the important thing is adjacent to the artifact.

### Where the brief was narrower than its own words

*"If something is wrong, say so plainly with the evidence."* — good, and I did. But the brief provided **no route for a finding that is not about the build**. The 28-day funding hole was found while verifying a *different* spec's arithmetic, and nothing in JOB 1 asked for it. It landed only because the reporting convention has a free-form summary. **A "findings outside scope" slot in the review brief would have made that a first-class output rather than an aside.**

### Constraint pair that nearly conflicted, and the hatch

**"Never build Debug — `bin\Debug` is the live collector"** against **"verify-gate prepush ⇒ GATE PASSED."** Verifying a build normally means building it. The hatch: `verify-gate.ps1` is **Release-only** (`dotnet build $proj -c Release` at line 50 — I checked before running it, precisely because getting this wrong stomps live collection). Worth stating in the next brief so the reviewer does not hesitate or, worse, skip the gate. **Related, and unresolved:** something *did* rebuild Debug at 2026-07-30 17:45 UTC during this window, and the collector was down for ~16 h afterwards. I did not establish a causal link and am not asserting one.

## 4. What I did not verify, and cannot

- **No live run of anything.** No trade was observed going from the WS stream to disk; the first flush, the first month rollover on real data and the first gap-repair HTTP call are all unexercised. **That is the trader's test gate**, and for this build it is the one that matters.
- **No AWS verification at all.** Exe-relative store resolution, once-on-start repair after a real restart, and the D1 single-box topology are reasoned and fixture-backed but unobserved on the deployed box. Under D1 that box is the *only* capturer.
- **No network call in any fixture.** By design — `HistoricalStore` is unlinked in the harness. A48d tests the resume *decision*, not the fetch; Deribit's refusal behaviour past ~24 h is untested by anyone.
- **The June wipe's cause.** I established the facts precisely — funding-only code changes, the mtime sequence, the 42,000-minute identity, 1m untouched — and **deliberately stopped short of diagnosing it**. I also noted the ~20 h survivor window resembles `gap_repair_lookback_hours: 20` and explicitly declined to assert a link, since gap repair has no candle path.
- **Whether A51 would catch a reintroduced overwrite.** I read what the fixtures assert; I did not re-introduce the bug and re-run.
- **The other six overlay whitelist blocks.** I audited `network.` and `auto_run.` because they looked suspicious; both failed. The implementer reports the remaining six audited clean. **I did not re-audit that audit** — and given a 2-of-2 failure rate on the ones I did check, a second pair of eyes on the other six is worth more than the usual.
- **The degraded-feed fallback rate**, which is unmeasurable until J-E ships — and which, per the JOB 2 packet §4, makes the matched-replay residual a lower bound rather than an estimate.
- **The pre-damage June bars.** The originals are gone (`backtest_data/` is gitignored), so "the re-fetch returned the same data" rests on every derivation figure reproducing, not on a diff.
